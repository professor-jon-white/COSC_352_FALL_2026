Question 1: Which 311 problems track violent crime?

Across Baltimore's neighborhoods, which types of 311 service request rise and fall most closely with violent crime, even after accounting for how many 311 requests each neighborhood makes in total? These are the neglect signals crews could prioritise first.

Finding. Across 268 neighborhoods, total 311 volume has a very strong positive relationship with violent crime (rho = 0.76), largely because busy neighborhoods report more of everything. After removing that size effect, the request type that tracks violent crime most closely is "HLTH-Animal Police/Fire/Sheriff/City Work Crew Standby" (rho = 0.44, 596 requests).

Question 2: Do liquor licenses track night-time violent crime?

Do neighborhoods with more active liquor licenses see more violent crime between 9 PM and 4 AM (Eastern time)?

Finding. Across 268 neighborhoods, active liquor licenses and night-time violent crime show a moderate positive relationship (rho = 0.46). Neighborhoods in the "6+ licenses" tier average 15.8 night-time violent crimes, versus 3.9 in the "0 licenses" tier. Night-time makes up 35.6% of violent crime with a known time in the "6+ licenses" tier, versus 29.6% in the "0 licenses" tier.

Question 3: Do high-crime neighborhoods wait longer for 311 service?

Neighborhoods are split into four groups by their level of violent crime. How long does it take the city to close a 311 request in each group, and how many requests are still open?

Finding. Neighborhoods in the highest violent-crime group close 311 requests in a median of 1.3 days, versus 2.2 days in the lowest group. Across 268 neighborhoods, violent crime and median close time show a weak negative relationship (rho = -0.11).

Question 4: Are licensed blocks more violent than unlicensed blocks?

Joining the three datasets at the level of individual street blocks: how much violent crime does the average block with a liquor license see compared with a block without one, and which license types stand out?

Finding. The 805 street blocks with a liquor license average 1.42 violent crimes per block, versus 0.25 on the 22,650 blocks without one (5.7x as many). The license type with the highest average is "Arena" (4.33 violent crimes per block).
