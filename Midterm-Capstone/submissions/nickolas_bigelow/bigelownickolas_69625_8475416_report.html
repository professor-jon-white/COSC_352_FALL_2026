Q3Department of Public Works & Department of Housing and Community Development

Do vacant buildings drive trash, dumping and rodent complaints, and where is demand out of line with vacancy?

Datasets: Vacant Building Notices + 311 Service Requests
Finding. Yes, with a strong link: neighborhoods with more open vacant notices file more sanitation, dumping and rodent requests (Pearson r = 0.94, Spearman = 0.93). Each additional vacant building is associated with about 2.25 more sanitation requests a year, and neighborhoods above the median vacancy level file 3.3x as many such requests as those below it. Druid Heights files the most sanitation requests beyond what its vacancy level predicts.

Q4Department of Transportation & 311 service agencies

Are 311 requests from high-crime neighborhoods resolved as quickly as those from elsewhere?

Datasets: 311 Service Requests + Part 1 Crime
Finding. Requests from the highest-crime third of neighborhoods take a median 5.6 days to close versus 3.9 days in the lowest-crime third, and 54.5% vs 69.0% are closed by their due date. After adjusting for request type, high-crime neighborhoods wait longer (index 1.14 vs 0.82), so the gap is not just a different mix of requests. The widest agency gap is at Forestry (+12.2 days).
